## Mined Tommy material

- **People are already going.** Companies are opening the door, and what he keeps hearing is that people go anyway, policy or not. Shadow IT is Mike's term. Shadow AI is the morph. Copilot is not given, or it is not enough, and the person knows what ChatGPT or Claude can do, so they use it. Two populations: the user, and, increasingly, leadership building their own data stuff. A marketing pamphlet IT never handed them is the old version. Communicating with data is the next one. He flags that for another episode. That episode is 583. Do not steal it.
- **IT's easiest sentence.** If it is not secure, we cannot, we have not vetted it, hold tight. Most people will not hold tight. He is hearing fewer "we will build our own agent" plans and more "we will just buy subscriptions," and plenty of orgs are still on "we do not want the data public, we are not doing Foundry, we are still vetting."
- **The wife story is the org.** His sister invites them for Thanksgiving. His wife says yes. He will not commit until the car, the travel, and the rest of the ducks are in a row, and then he tells her not to say yes yet because you cannot say yes and backtrack. That is how he watches companies evaluate AI. He is the one blocking the yes.
- **Refusal is a cost, not a safety win.** A personal subscription is a security risk. He grants that. The data goes to their server. Refusal has a hidden cost: disgruntled people whose friends' companies let them use it, and the approved tool is the one that does not actually work. "We're still vetting" is not free.
- **This is the Power BI movie.** Ten years ago Power BI was shadow IT for a lot of orgs, especially the first five years, because anyone could build a report and it was not on the approved list. The bone of BI is communicating information. The medium changes. SSRS did not stop people going back to the source. Least path of resistance is why managed self-service exists in the shape it does. Organizations could not stop people building their own reports. For anyone young: BI used to be owned by IT. You wanted a report, you waited on the SQL Server team. We left that.
- **The new hold.** Ask the BI team, take a discovery call, wait out a build and revisions. Or open a Claude dashboard, maybe pointed at the source, and get something that communicates. He calls that a choice, and the word shadow shows up when you are talking about risk. Two risks he names: information shared publicly (publish to web, no funnel, no experience), and communicating the wrong information.
- **Buying everyone a subscription repeats the early Power BI mistake.** He asks Mike what happened when orgs just handed out Power BI while it was still shadow IT and said go create your own stuff. The consequence he is aiming at: it works until someone asks how you got here. A personal process with AI is the same as a personal process with BI. Software, a marketing pamphlet, a dashboard. Someone leaves or changes seats and the org cannot repeat it or trust it. "We'll pay for your subscription" is a fine temporary output. It does not leave you with the prompts, a skill, or an owner. Who owns the prompts a person wrote on their own subscription?
- **The FTE story.** A strong teammate at FTE left suddenly. The report was on the service and they could not download it. He had done something with XMLA. Someone rebuilt the bookmarks and the rest, step by step. That is the concern he is mapping onto AI. One person holds the process. You cannot recreate the bookmarks.
- **Minimum bar, and he ties it to distributed wisdom.** If you hand the tool out, you also need a priority, a task force, on how the knowledge is shared. At the very least a place to post the skill and the prompt. Dashboard or pamphlet, if the company is using it, it goes on the SharePoint page. Not the ad-hoc one-off. Otherwise the pamphlets were great until Kevin or Susan left. He does not want to be doom. He wants the BI lesson applied forward. Same issue if you are the developer or the person consuming the data.
- **BI people are the ones set up for this.** Question he has been stewing on: can the lessons of shadow BI and shadow IT, the Wild West, the governance, the shared wisdom, and the enabling, become the framework for how an org uses AI? Or is he just forcing his own experience onto it? His current answer: not a perfect one-to-one, and the overlap is larger than people think. BI had to be the face of the data and the face of the business. You did not survive by living in the corner after you published the report. You already spent years pulling shadow BI back in. A software developer who only lives on the back end did not have that job.
- **AI center of excellence.** Same shape as the Power BI one. AI champions. Shared prompts. Training. Process. Not "here is Claude, go build," because that grows roots you cannot see. Enable people and keep an overwatch so a person and the company do not drift. Ideally everyone moves the same direction.

**Plot:** good close. As its own post it collides with the CoE piece and with "you're AI ready."
