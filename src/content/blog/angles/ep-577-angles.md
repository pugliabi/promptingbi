## Mined Tommy material

- **Elephant:** everyone says they need governance or already have it. Ask them what it is and they cannot say, and they cannot name the people in the process. The common misconception is security and access control. He puts that at about 15% of a program that actually works (later he says the 20% slice people think is the whole pie). First problem: you may think you can do it. Can you implement it, and what does success look like?
- **You do not finish Fabric.** "We finished Power BI, we finished Fabric" is the line he does not buy. Maturity without governance in place, and without leadership and the teams treating it as a priority, is the doomed path. Three questions he wanted on the table: what it actually is, can you succeed without it, what happens if leadership does not buy in.
- **Clients open with the security list.** "Oh, you do data governance. Great. Here's all the security list." His read: you are missing the role, the goal, and the mission.
- **His definition, offered as the template he uses:** data governance is the enablement and empowerment of data and users through roles, responsibilities, and initiatives. Security, access, and change management sit inside that. Simply put, it is enablement and empowerment of people connecting to and using their data. The work may happen outside the governance meeting. The goals and initiatives are the governance. It is a journey because the initiatives change. Someone has to decide, someone has to carry the decision out, someone has to say whether it worked. Big projects and the day-to-day both count. Engineering through to the consumer.
- **Why the security-only version feels like IT.** It is not attractive. It sounds like IT security. What he wants in the definition is people, goals, and policy: apps and a different way to consume, a better path from requesting a report to getting the information, a way to track a member across systems. Active participation, or it does not work. He "never understood why they didn't understand it" until he saw they thought governance was the security slice and then you just run.
- **What they cannot digest.** Workspace plus a policy, this report lives here, only these people can open it, done. Then you say you need commitment from every team and from leadership, a budget, and an ongoing effort. "Whoa. There's this much work involved. There's a budget involved. Wait, I thought this was just a security thing. Not that I have to go to a committee meeting." People are why it succeeds, not the technology.
- **Lack of budget, and he means an invoice.** Not a vibe that "we'll spend some money." An invoice: this many hours from these teams this quarter, and this cost for the initiatives you said mattered. He has not seen a program work without that budget in place. Initiatives get off the ground and go nowhere if the invoice is not accepted. Creating that budget is on whoever wants governance. Two budgets inside it: time (five hours a week of a team that would otherwise not be doing this, five hours of leadership in a room) and actual dollars (the tracking technology, the R&D for member accounts). He rephrases Mike's "money is time" into lack of budget: both the hours and the scratch, written down.
- **Lack of direction.** Second thing they cannot digest. We want governance, but what is the long-term goal, who is involved, and why would anyone still want to do this in three months? Committees that meet and do nothing fall apart. Short term (the security fixes) and twelve months out (a full picture of each customer, the customer journey) have to be the same direction or people cannot commit. "It's the only time I'm happy I had a sociology degree." Nobody does this 40 hours a week. You still have to get people moving the same way and staying responsible so the right reports get to people. Lip service is "we just want better trust in the reports." Without the direction and the commitment, it stops.
- **Lack of owning ownership. Two layers.** Definitions, and the initiative. A client (he shouts them out, no name) has seven definitions of the same field, years in, and still cannot decide. "That's smoke and fire at the same time." BI cannot own bad data coming in. Someone changes a part or a color and the answer is "oops, next time we'll do better." He will not take that. "That's what an excuse my kids give. And I don't even accept that." Second layer: for a goal or initiative, who owns it? Committee lead versus the person executing. Wanting Fabric apps is not a plan. If it never ships, who is responsible? If it does, reward them. Day-to-day definitions and change management, plus the long-term initiatives. Both are "owning ownership."
- **Joke he wants:** organizations are "lactose intolerant" around data governance. The stomach metaphor is his. Do not sand it into "change readiness."
- **Monday, his version, not Mike's roadmap.** You cannot eat the whole pie. Life hack: leadership, or a representative from each department, in a room. Workshop the pain points and the goals. Find the pattern everyone shares (data not arriving in a relevant way, nobody agrees what a product rollup is). That gets people facing the same direction. Then you can talk budget, because now there is something they all said they wanted, and it will take time. Close: most organizations cannot stomach it because of lack of direction, lack of budget, and lack of owning ownership.

## Other angles

### You Do Not Get to Finish Fabric
**Thesis:** "We finished the rollout" is the sentence that means governance never started. Maturity without it, and without leadership treating it as a priority, does not hold.
**Plot:** short. It is the hook of the three-lacks post, not a second article. Collides with the step-zero post if it turns into another adoption-roadmap essay.

### Org Apps Are the Container, Rayfin Is the Action
**Thesis:** An org app can hold the Power BI report and the Fabric app as the next tab. The report answers the weekly question. The Rayfin side is writeback, uploads, and actions. They have to share a theme or it feels like two products.
**Plot:** news. He says the org-app story and Project Osmos each deserve their own conversation. Park the Rayfin half next to episode 578 so it does not get written twice.

### Project Osmos Keeps the Task After You Disconnect
**Thesis:** The interesting part is not another coding agent. It is long-running stateful tasks hosted in Fabric, so context, history, and project knowledge survive after the harness disconnects. He wants to know whether that context lives in Fabric or on his laptop.
**Plot:** news-reaction. He has not experimented yet. Stale fast. Do not write the mechanism past what he said.
