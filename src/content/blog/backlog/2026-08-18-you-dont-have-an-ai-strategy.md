**OUTLINE, not a draft.** Companion post to [You're AI Ready. Your Organization Isn't.](/2026/08/12/youre-ai-ready-your-organization-isnt/), which diagnoses the definition gap. This one is the other failure mode: plenty of motion, no direction. Keep the two separate. That post gets one passing mention of theater and does not spend the term.

## Angle

There's a difference between AI strategy and AI theater, and right now I see a lot of theater and very little strategy. Theater is a solution wandering the halls looking for a problem. Strategy starts from the company's goals and its worst pain points, and pilots against them in small doses.

## Sections to write

**Theater defined.** Microsoft announced something, so we should do that too. Let's spin up a Copilot agent and call it an initiative. Reacting to a headline is not a strategy, it's a press release with a budget attached. Contrast frame throughout: theater versus strategy.

**The tell: nobody can name the team or the pain.** If an AI initiative can't say which team it serves and which pain it removes, it's theater. Reverse the usual order of questions. It's almost never "which model." It's "what are we actually trying to achieve with our data?"

**It runs on YOUR data, which makes this a governance question.** An agent in your company runs on your data, so the starting point is data governance and data quality: what are our priorities around data, what analysis do people need to make decisions. That's what surfaces where agents could actually apply. Also the ownership answer: AI enablement belongs to the data governance committee, because IT, BI, and the purse strings are already in that room. (Careful: the readiness post already makes the ownership point. Here it should land as the antidote to theater specifically, not a repeat.)

**Where to look instead: your report request backlog.** The best tell for agentic candidates is where reporting pain already lives. Where do report requests still pile up? Who re-keys the same information every week? Operations staring down the same filled-out forms. Those pain points bridge almost directly to something agentic, because the need is real and the data path already exists. Contrast against the theater version: image generation and pamphlet agents. "Cool. Don't need it."

**Small doses.** Not everyone adopts at once, and that's fine. Power BI didn't roll out to everyone on day one either. POC, one team, one process, evaluate honestly, then decide whether more agents add value or just add spend.

**Close.** Prediction: the theater wave will produce a lot of decommissioned agents in 18 months, and the companies that started from a pain point will be the ones still running theirs. Key takeaway: name the team and the pain before you name the tool.
